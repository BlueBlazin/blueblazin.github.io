Explain Lost Evidence And Outcomes

By the end of this hour

  • Trace a task failure to a specific missing piece of evidence.
  • Separate observed omissions from causal claims.

Before you begin

  • Use the paired navigation results and saved selection traces from Activity 6, step 3.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

A useful failure analysis names the exact evidence required, where it existed, when it was omitted, and what the agent did without it. Missing evidence can explain a failure, but a trace alone may not prove causality: the model might still fail when shown the information. Distinguish observation from interpretation. A targeted development replay with the missing excerpt restored can strengthen the explanation, provided it is labeled a diagnostic intervention rather than another trial under the original policy. Keep final tasks out of this debugging loop.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    Distinguish these statements: “the definition was absent from context” and “the missing definition caused the incorrect edit.”

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Spend 10 minutes on H19: Inspect evaluation traces for tool-use failures. Stop at the time limit and use the page as a reference while building.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Write reports/navigation-analysis.md with a compact timeline: task requirement, required file/lines, search or read request, returned evidence, omission, action, and grade. Analyze one success and one failure where available. If all runs pass, construct a small development diagnostic that lowers a retrieval cap enough to omit a known dependency and label it as an injected case. Optionally restore only the missing excerpt for one diagnostic replay. Save both traces and explain what the replay supports without treating a single stochastic difference as proof.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Check every factual claim against a trace or file snapshot. End the note with one narrowly scoped policy change to test later and the evidence that motivated it.

What to save

Written evidence-loss analysis linked to traces.

Watch for: Do not invent an information-loss failure if the saved runs provide no such case; create and label a controlled diagnostic instead.

Check your understanding

Why label an excerpt-restoration replay as diagnostic?

Check your reasoning

It changes the original policy’s information and is designed to investigate a mechanism. Mixing it into the original comparison would alter the experimental condition after observing outcomes.
