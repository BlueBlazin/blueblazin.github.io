Generate Claims From Audited Evidence

By the end of this hour

  • Write conclusions no stronger than the audited evidence.
  • Separate future experiments from the frozen reported result.

Before you begin

  • Grader audit, cost reconciliation, and task-grouped uncertainty summary.
  • The unchanged experiment manifest and failure analysis.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

A useful limitations section explains what the study could not distinguish and why that matters. Here the scope includes a small custom task suite, a pinned model, particular context limits, a few repeats, and your specific implementations. Audit findings may narrow the supported conclusion further. Separate observations, interpretations, and proposed follow-ups so a reader can see which claims are measured. A negative or uncertain result still demonstrates sound engineering when the experiment is reproducible and its limits are honestly described.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    State your result in one sentence using the measured task suite, model, and budget. Remove any claim broader than those conditions.

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Spend 5 minutes on H8: Limit conclusions to evaluated tasks and budgets. Spend 5 minutes on H4: Separate reliability measures from chosen examples. Stop at the time limit and use the page as a reference while building.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Create a small findings generator that reads the audited tables and emits each predeclared metric with numerator, denominator, task count, model/configuration identity, budget, and interval method. Add one assertion that incomplete runs cannot be labeled a complete96-attempt study, and another that a changed task weighting is detected. Compare the generated values with the paired task differences. Write a short interpretation stating what the data resolves and one follow-up hypothesis; use the Day 0 report template for presentation later. Preserve frozen results.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Save the findings and limitations alongside the frozen result tables. Confirm report numbers agree with the latest documented audit version.

What to save

Tested findings generator, one supported claim, and a follow-up hypothesis.

Watch for: Do not frame a development-pilot improvement as a held-out final result.

Check your understanding

Can an inconclusive comparison still be a strong portfolio project?

Check your reasoning

Yes. Clear controls, complete accounting, reproducible code, and honest uncertainty show engineering and research judgment independent of a positive headline.
