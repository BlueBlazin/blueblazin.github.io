Launch The Second Matched Trial Round

By the end of this hour

  • Run the second matched repeat without outcome-driven changes.
  • Retain failures, timeouts, and actual usage in the study records.

Before you begin

  • First-round status records and the immutable trial manifest.
  • The original frozen code/configuration and remaining study budget.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

Repeated trials expose variability in the same task-condition setting. They are meaningful only if the second round uses the same rules as the first. Compare versions and limits before launching, then record the second round as additional planned evidence rather than a chance to repair unfavorable outcomes. A trial that reaches its cost or time cap remains an attempted failure. Keep operational progress separate from scientific results so partially completed jobs do not silently vanish when a reporting script selects only rows with a success field.

  3. Build

Verify the frozen revision and configuration hashes again, then launch repeat two for both conditions across all sixteen tasks. Use the same execution-order policy and limits. Check that grader failures and model/tool failures are recorded distinctly while both remain unsuccessful task outcomes when appropriate. Update cumulative usage and cost, including failed calls and compaction. If the predeclared study cap prevents launch, stop and mark unattempted rows explicitly rather than hiding them.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Save a second-round status table for 32 rows and a cumulative accounting summary. Preserve raw traces and pending statuses for background work.

What to save

Next 32 attempted trials retain all outcomes.

Watch for: Do not filter the analysis input to only trials that produced a normal final answer.

Check your understanding

Are a grader execution error and a validly graded task failure the same diagnostic category?

Check your reasoning

No. Both may be unsuccessful attempts under the study rules, but the former is a measurement/infrastructure problem and the latter a task outcome; retain the distinction.

Complete Trials and Verify Outcome Accounting

By the end of this hour

  • Launch the third repeat and reconcile the complete ninety-six-attempt study.
  • Produce an outcome table linked to raw evidence.

Before you begin

  • First and second repeat manifests, traces, and usage records.
  • The third-repeat rows and the unchanged frozen experiment.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

The final reconciliation asks whether every planned trial has an honest status and an evidence trail. A completed study has ninety-six attempted rows, not merely ninety-six filenames. Check task and condition counts, terminal outcomes, grader results, usage, and trace paths. Some infrastructure interruptions may prevent a complete study within the predeclared budget; if that happens, report the shortfall explicitly rather than claiming ninety-six attempts. The table becomes the authoritative analysis input, while raw traces remain available for later audit and explanation.

Check your understanding

What should you report if only ninety attempts fit the frozen total-study cap?

Check your reasoning

Report ninety attempts and six unattempted rows with the stopping reason. Do not claim a complete ninety-six-attempt study or silently change the denominator.
