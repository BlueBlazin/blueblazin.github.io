Validate Evaluation Result Tables

By the end of this hour

  • Validate the scope and denominators of every reported comparison.
  • Label optional external evidence accurately and retain all its outcomes.

Before you begin

  • Required private-study tables and trace-linked failure analysis.
  • Optional external pilot records or the documented skip decision.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

Results from different suites should remain identifiable. The private study has sixteen tasks and three repeats per condition; the optional external pilot has ten tasks and one repeat per condition. Pooling them casually changes task weighting and hides differences in infrastructure and difficulty. This session checks labels, denominators, attempted counts, and costs before the report grows around them. A transparent incomplete pilot is more useful than a polished table that drops failures or suggests benchmark-wide validity from a small convenience subset.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    State the private and external task/repeat counts and explain why their success percentages should not be pooled without a declared method.

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Spend 5 minutes on H17: Parametrized validation of missing/duplicate result rows. Spend 5 minutes on H4: Keep trial metrics and suite scope explicit. Stop at the time limit and use the page as a reference while building.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Implement an analysis-input validator that joins the frozen manifest with trial rows and asserts unique IDs, expected task/condition membership, attempted versus unattempted states, compatible versions, known units, and explicit missing usage. Test a duplicate row, a missing trial, a renamed condition, and a failed trial lacking usage. Keep private and optional external results in separate tables with their own planned/attempted counts. Run the validator on the saved study and attach its output to the analysis. If the optional pilot was skipped, its absence is a valid labeled state.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Save a one-page analysis-scope note with a private/optional-external comparison table and corrected report labels.

What to save

Audited private-task result table and negative input tests.

Watch for: Do not describe the optional subset score as a Terminal-Bench leaderboard result.

Check your understanding

Why can combining the two suites distort the result?

Check your reasoning

They have different task counts, repeat counts, and environments. Unplanned pooling changes the effective weighting and may conceal suite-specific behavior.
