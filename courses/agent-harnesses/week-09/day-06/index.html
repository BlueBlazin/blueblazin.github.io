Reconcile Run Budgets And Actual Charges

By the end of this hour

  • Reconcile reported trial costs with usage and billing evidence.
  • Separate reservations, estimates, and known actual charges.

Before you begin

  • Per-call usage logs, per-trial cost tables, and budget-reservation events.
  • Provider billing/export data available to your account, with credentials kept private.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

Budget reservations and actual charges serve different purposes. A reservation limits possible spending before a call; usage records and billing describe what was consumed afterward. Retries, failed requests, caching, and compaction may make a naive input-plus-output estimate diverge from the bill. Audit the fields your provider actually exposes and label unresolved amounts. The goal is to explain the difference between the run’s enforced cap and the reported cost, while retaining unsuccessful attempts and any known charges from interrupted calls.

  3. Build

Watch for: Do not publish account identifiers, API keys, or private billing details in the portfolio.

Check your understanding

Why can a cost cap be respected while the exact billed amount remains uncertain?

Check your reasoning

The harness can conservatively reserve against a cap, while interrupted responses or delayed provider billing leave the final consumed amount unresolved until later.

Calculate Uncertainty Across Paired Task Results

By the end of this hour

  • Estimate the paired task-level success difference.
  • Compute uncertainty without treating repeated trials as independent tasks.

Before you begin

  • Audited trial outcomes with task and condition identifiers.
  • The uncertainty method predeclared in experiment-plan.md.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

Trials on the same task share difficulty and structure, so treating all ninety-six observations as unrelated can overstate precision. First calculate each condition’s mean success across its three trials on each task, then compute the paired difference for that task. Average those sixteen differences for a transparent task-weighted estimate. A task-cluster bootstrap resamples task IDs and carries all their paired trial records together. Its interval describes uncertainty under those assumptions; a small custom suite still cannot establish performance on all coding tasks or future model versions. Task-level resampling also assumes tasks are adequate sampling units. If multiple tasks share a generator or closely related family, disclose this dependence and treat the interval as a descriptive task-bootstrap summary; it does not correct family-level correlation or establish population coverage.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    Explain why resampling individual trials breaks the task grouping. State what stays together in a task-cluster sample.

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Spend 10 minutes on H20: Paired indices, analysis RNG, and degenerate bootstrap data. Stop at the time limit and use the page as a reference while building.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Compute per-task mean success for each condition and their paired differences using the audited table. Follow the predeclared uncertainty method; if it is a task-cluster bootstrap, resample the sixteen task IDs with replacement using a fixed analysis seed, retaining both conditions and every repeat for each sampled task. Use enough inexpensive resamples for a stable descriptive interval and record the count. Save the point estimate, interval, and per-task differences. Explain the effect of missing attempts according to the frozen accounting rule and state the small-suite limitations. Before bootstrapping, check completeness. Preserve attempted/planned counts and follow the predeclared incomplete-study rule; a partial study remains descriptive and is not the complete 96-attempt estimate.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Check the mean paired difference against the direct equal-task-weighted comparison. Save analysis code, seed, interval method, and a concise interpretation.

What to save

Per-task clustered analysis and uncertainty summary.

Watch for: Do not choose a new uncertainty method only because it produces a more favorable interval.

Check your understanding

Does an interval spanning zero prove that the policies are equivalent?

Check your reasoning

No. It indicates that this study does not resolve the direction of the difference at that uncertainty level; equivalence requires its own margin and design.
