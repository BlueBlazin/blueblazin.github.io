Derive And Implement Rotary Positions

By the end of this hour

  • Implement rotary positions with explicit absolute offsets.
  • Verify the identity and relative-position dot-product properties.

Before you begin

  • The modern block with RMSNorm and SwiGLU.
  • Attention Q/K tensors with an even head dimension.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

RoPE rotates pairs of query and key channels by position-dependent angles. For each pair, R(θ)[a,b]=[a cosθ−b sinθ, a sinθ+b cosθ]. At position zero the rotation is identity. Applying rotations at positions m and n yields (R_m q)ᵀ(R_n k)=qᵀR_(n−m)k, exposing relative position in the attention score. Frequencies vary across channel pairs. Your pairing layout and frequency convention must be explicit: mathematically valid alternatives can still be incompatible with another implementation’s weights.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    Write the 2×2 rotation matrix and calculate what it does at angles zero and π/2.

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Read A3’s rotary construction and relative-position identity. Derive R_mᵀR_n=R_(n−m) for one pair before generalizing to a head.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Choose an adjacent-pair channel layout and an explicit frequency base. Build cosine and sine values from a supplied positions vector rather than assuming every call begins at zero. Apply rotation to Q and K, preserving [B,H,T,D], and leave V unchanged. Test identity at zero, norm preservation and the relative-position dot-product equation using random vectors. Include an offset example with positions 7 through 10 and a negative relative difference in the algebraic test. Keep learned-position removal for the integration decision explicit in your notes.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Save the RoPE function, its layout/frequency specification and numerical errors for the identity tests. Use FP32 CPU inputs first.

What to save

Position-zero identity and relative-position dot-product tests pass.

Watch for: Do not silently mix adjacent-pair and split-half channel conventions between Q, K or a reference implementation.

Check your understanding

Does the relative-position identity mean that rotating Q alone is sufficient?

Check your reasoning

No. The cancellation to a relative rotation uses both R_m on Q and R_n on K. Rotating only one side leaves a score tied to that side’s absolute position.
