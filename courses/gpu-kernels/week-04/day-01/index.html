Fuse One Matmul Output Epilogue

By the end of this hour

  • Fuse a column-bias epilogue into the validated matmul.
  • Match the reference’s rounding semantics before timing.

Before you begin

  • Your validated matmul kernel and one frozen tile configuration.
  • A length-N bias vector and the existing boundary-shape matrix.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

An epilogue transforms the accumulator before it is written to global memory. Adding a bias inside the matmul can avoid a separate launch and an extra read/write of the intermediate output. However, adding bias to an FP32 accumulator and then casting is not numerically identical to first rounding matmul to FP16 and then adding bias. Define the intended operation before comparing implementations. A bias indexed by output column has shape N and broadcasts across rows. Its tail mask must follow the output-column validity. The objective is one correct fused epilogue, not a collection of activation variants.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    Write the formula and shape for a per-column bias. Mark where your current matmul casts its accumulator.

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Read K3’s matmul epilogue example and identify how transformations occur before the final store. Choose bias only for this required exercise.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Add an optional length-N bias input to a separate fused entry point, retaining the original plain matmul. Load bias by output column under the column mask and add it to the FP32 accumulator before the final cast. Define a reference that matches that rounding order explicitly. Test zero bias, varying positive/negative bias, and existing M/N/K boundary shapes; ensure a nonconstant bias exposes wrong-axis broadcasting. Record how this semantics differs from a separately rounded FP16 matmul-plus-bias expression. Save the passing error table before collecting any performance measurements.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Verify the zero-bias path agrees with plain matmul within the established criterion. Save the fused support contract and its explicit reference expression.

What to save

Fused output matches its unfused reference on supported shapes.

Watch for: Do not attribute a large wrong-axis bias error to ordinary floating-point rounding.

Check your understanding

Why can a fused matmul-plus-bias differ from two separate FP16 operations without being incorrect?

Check your reasoning

Fusion can add bias before the intermediate matmul result is rounded; the separate expression may round between operations.
