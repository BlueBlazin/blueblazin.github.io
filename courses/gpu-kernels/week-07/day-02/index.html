Trace Hopper Pipelined Matmul Stages

By the end of this hour

  • Trace the stages of the official Hopper matmul pipeline.
  • Explain buffer reuse and synchronization through a concrete tile.

Before you begin

  • K6 official Hopper pipelined matmul source.
  • Your annotated memory-space notes from step 11-2.

Environment or workflow question? Day 0 handbook.

The key idea

Pipelined matmul overlaps movement of future tiles with computation on current tiles, but overlap is safe only when producer and consumer ordering is explicit. A shared-memory buffer cannot be overwritten while an outstanding computation still reads it. Multiple stages trade extra storage for opportunities to hide transfer latency, and the final drain must finish outstanding work before results are used. This session studies the official implementation as written. It does not require writing a new matmul kernel or reproducing headline throughput on the rental GPU.

Your 60-minute plan

  1. Retrieve

    5 min

    Sketch an unpipelined load–compute–load–compute sequence and identify the idle periods.

  2. Study

    10 min

    Use the core resources above for ten minutes in total, focusing only on the indicated sections. Read K6 Hopper pipelined matmul, following one K-dimension tile and its synchronization rather than every tuning option.

  3. Build

    35 min

    Create a table with columns for iteration, tile being transferred, tile being computed, buffer index, and event that permits buffer reuse. Fill it for the first few iterations and the final drain of the official example using its actual stage count. Annotate where global-memory data enters shared memory, where computation consumes it, and where results are committed. Explain which waits protect readiness and which protect reuse. Compare this schedule with your simpler Triton matmul from the earlier module. Keep the H100 off unless a narrowly necessary smoke check is already budgeted.

  4. Verify and save

    10 min

    Save the pipeline table and one paragraph explaining startup, steady state, and drain, linked to the exact official source version.

What to save

Annotated pipeline identifying stages and buffer lifetimes.

Watch for: Marking a buffer reusable as soon as its transfer finishes rather than after its consumers finish.

Check your understanding

Why can adding more pipeline stages reduce performance?

Check your reasoning

Additional buffers consume limited on-chip storage and can reduce other forms of parallelism or add overhead; overlap is not free.
